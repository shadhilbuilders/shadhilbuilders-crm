## Problem Statement

The project work dashboard at `/[orgSlug]/projects/[projectSlug]/dashboard` is a read-only metrics page:
a KPI strip, six charts on the manager branch (plus an inline status list on the staff branch), and a
HOLD-bookings table. No single user sees all seven; the "7 charts" figure in R1 was wrong. It shows
numbers about work. It cannot do work. Every action a staff member needs — call a lead, move its state,
schedule a visit, log an outcome — lives on a different page.

The owner's ask: no charts; one dashboard where every work role completes every action without
navigating; other pages stay in place; and because all information at once would be messy, show it
progressively.

The diagnostic finding that reframes that ask: Shadhil's telecallers and sales execs do their real work
in Excel or a notebook plus WhatsApp on personal phones, and treat this CRM as tolerated data entry. The
dashboard's competitor is not another CRM. It is a habit. A page that displays metrics loses to a tool
that does the job, no matter how good the metrics are.

## Demand Evidence

Honest accounting. Demand for the product is settled — Shadhil is a paying client with `DESIGN.md` v3.1
signed off. Demand for *this change* is the owner's instruction, not observed staff behaviour.

What is evidenced is the problem, not the solution:

- Owner-stated (2026-09-16): staff run the pipeline from Excel/notebook + WhatsApp; the CRM is a
  data-entry tax they tolerate.
- `T-ADOPT` (weekly adoption telemetry, DAU per role) is still open
  (`docs/planning/IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN-v1.md:2089`), so nobody has measured adoption. The workaround is
  reported, not instrumented.
- The owner confirmed he has never watched a staff member use the CRM without helping them.

So: the pain is real and named by the client; the fix is unvalidated. That gap is why the assignment at
the end of this doc is an observation, not a build step.

## Status Quo

Concrete current workflow, as reported:

1. Message the customer from personal WhatsApp. That is where the actual conversation happens.
2. Keep the pipeline in Excel or a paper notebook — name, phone, what was said, when to call back.
3. Open the CRM afterwards to record the outcome, retyping what was already typed in WhatsApp.
4. For a visit, coordinate on WhatsApp and enter the visit in the CRM separately.

Cost: every interaction is entered twice, the CRM's state is stale by design, the 30-minute first-touch
SLA is unenforced, and management gets no visibility until someone asks for the register.

Root cause verified in code, not assumed:

- **Chat send never reaches the customer.** `apps/web/src/hooks/queries/crm.ts:504` hardcodes
  `channel: 'IN_APP'` in `useSendMessage`. The backend enqueues a WhatsApp outbound message only in the
  `channel === 'WHATSAPP'` branch (`apps/backend/src/chat/chat.service.ts:254`). A reply composed in the
  CRM therefore does not arrive on the customer's phone. Staff are correct to stay in WhatsApp.
- **Reminders are inert.** `apps/backend/src/reminders/reminders.service.ts:68-71` — `deliverReminder` is
  `// No-op until Week 7. return;` (function at 68, `return;` at 70). The cron claims rows, calls the
  no-op, and marks them `SENT`. No service and no seed ever creates a `Reminder` row: the table is empty
  by construction.
- **Inbound customer messages DO arrive in the CRM, and replies silently do not leave it.**
  `apps/backend/src/webhooks/webhooks.controller.ts:316` inserts inbound rows with `direction = 'IN'`,
  `channel = 'WHATSAPP'`, `userId = NULL`. So a customer's WhatsApp reply genuinely appears in the CRM
  chat pane, while a staff reply composed in that same pane is saved, renders as sent, and is never
  enqueued. The CRM presents a live customer message next to a reply box that appears to work and does
  not. That is worse than an absent feature: it is a false signal, and it is why the chat pane is
  excluded from this dashboard.
- **The telecaller's headline action is currently broken.** A telecaller can see "Schedule visit" on the
  existing lead detail page and the request fails with a 400. See Decision 1.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**All three work roles are served on this one page** — telecaller, sales exec, manager. What changes per
role is the hero panel and the permitted action on each row, not the route. The page is a work queue for
whoever is signed in: the queue is theirs, the counts are theirs, and the action on a row is one their
role is actually permitted to take.

**Hero: the telecaller.** Their job sits at the top of the funnel and carries the only hard, measurable
deadline in the system: a NEW lead is overdue 30 minutes after creation (`OVERDUE_AFTER_MIN`,
`lib/leads.ts:35`). A telecaller who misses it produces no visit, no booking, no revenue. The server
already orders overdue NEW first (`leads.service.ts:293-301`), evidence someone believed this was the
critical path before this redesign.

**Sales exec:** same page, queue of their VISITED / NEGOTIATION / BOOKING_INITIATED leads, and "today's
visits" becomes "log visit outcomes" — where they, and only they, mark a visit COMPLETED. This is the
role the lead hands over to at the visit outcome (Model C), so their panel is the second half of the
same funnel rather than a separate concern.

**Manager:** queue, today's visits, and the booking approval queue via `BookingApprovalDialog`. The
oversight role for the same work the other two are doing, not a separate reporting surface.

**Narrowest wedge (owner-selected scope A):** an ordered queue where every row shows its next action as
a real button, expanding in place to the full workspace, plus a "today's visits" panel, role-adapted per
the three views above. No chat-send fix, no reminders backend. Scope is the dashboard route plus the two
disclosed backend changes in Decision 1; the "no backend change" basis of scope A is retracted.

## Decision 1 (RESOLVED — owner ruling 2026-09-16) — telecaller schedules AND assigns the exec

Owner's ruling: a telecaller schedules the visit and assigns it to a sales exec drawn from their own team
members or team manager; the sales exec then changes the status to VISITED.

**The ruling resolves the 400 without any change to the create path — but tracing the described workflow
end to end found three linked breaks, and all three must be fixed or the workflow fails one step later.**

### Break 1 — the create 400. Fixable with NO backend change.

`visits.service.ts:225-244` resolves the assignee as `dto.salesExecId ?? actor.sub` and requires
SALES_EXEC or higher. A telecaller who **supplies** a valid `salesExecId` passes this check untouched. The
request fails today only because the picker never renders for them (`ScheduleVisitDialog.tsx:92`:
`showExecPicker = isAdminOrOwner || role === 'MANAGER'`) and the exec list returns `[]` for them
(`users.service.ts:1011-1013`).

**`GET /api/users/team` is the missing source, and it already exists and already works for telecallers.**
`users.service.ts:1088+` (`teamMembers()`) resolves, for ordinary staff, every team they belong to via
their own `TeamMember` rows, **plus each team's manager** — documented as "staff: team + manager"
(`users.controller.ts:217-223`). It is role-safe, RLS-correct, and already proven in production: it backs
the chat @mention picker.

**Fix (frontend only):** feed `ScheduleVisitDialog`'s exec field from `/api/users/team`, filtered to
`SALES_EXEC | MANAGER`, and always send `salesExecId`. This matches "within their teams member or team
manager" exactly, and needs no backend change.

### Break 2 — the exec cannot complete the handoff. NEEDS A NARROW BACKEND CHANGE.

An exec marks COMPLETED, which drives `VISIT_SCHEDULED → VISITED` (`visits.service.ts:477-480`). The lead
is still in VISIT_SCHEDULED at that moment, and `execLane` is
`['VISITED','NEGOTIATION','BOOKING_INITIATED']` (`leads.state-machine.ts:107-118`). `transition()` calls
`canTransition` and throws `ForbiddenException` (`leads.service.ts:1496-1509`). **A real SALES_EXEC
cannot perform the exec's own core action.**

The existing test masks this: the COMPLETED-to-VISITED baseline runs as ADMIN
(`visits.service.test.ts:200`), the only role whose gate passes. There is no SALES_EXEC coverage of the
handoff.

**Fix:** give SALES_EXEC exactly one extra edge, `VISIT_SCHEDULED → VISITED`, explicit and narrow. It must
NOT be done by adding VISIT_SCHEDULED to the lane wholesale: the lane reuses
`TRANSITIONS['VISIT_SCHEDULED'] = ['VISITED','NO_SHOW','RESCHEDULED','COLD','LOST']`, which would hand the
exec NO_SHOW / LOST / COLD and take them from the telecaller's re-engagement lane — contrary to Model C.
The same edit removes TELECALLER's `VISIT_SCHEDULED → VISITED` (filed defect 5), which is precisely the
owner's ruling.

### Break 3 — the exec cannot see the visit they are assigned. NEEDS A BACKEND CHANGE.

`visits.service.ts:137-138` scopes SALES_EXEC to `where['lead'] = { ownerId: actor.sub }` — visits whose
parent lead they **own**. Under the owner's model the lead stays with the telecaller until the visit
happens, so the assigned exec sees nothing, cannot find the visit, and cannot complete it. Breaks 2 and 3
together make the exec lane inert.

**Fix:** for SALES_EXEC, include visits **assigned** to them as well as those on leads they own — a union
on `userId = actor.sub OR lead.ownerId = actor.sub`. This also implements the "shared visibility window"
Model C describes and the code never built.

### Bonus defect found while tracing

`visits.service.ts:110` writes the `projectId` filter into `where['lead']`, then line 137 **overwrites**
`where['lead']` for TELECALLER / SALES_EXEC. The project filter is therefore silently discarded for staff
roles. A correctness bug, worth fixing in the same pass.

### Net scope impact, stated honestly

Decision 1 is resolved. The workflow the owner described requires **two backend changes** (Breaks 2 and 3),
not zero; Break 1 is frontend-only. The R1 claim that the whole lane needed no backend work was wrong, and
this is the real cost.

### Fork — RESOLVED by owner ruling 2026-09-16

**The telecaller assigns the VISIT to the exec. Lead OWNERSHIP does not transfer at scheduling time.**

Consequences, all good: no `canReassign` change (it stays ADMIN/MANAGER, `lib/session.ts:60-62`); the
telecaller keeps the lead in their queue and keeps Model C's NO_SHOW re-engagement lane; the exec gains
responsibility for the visit, not the record. The lifecycle the client signed off is untouched — this is
the outcome that required no design change at all.

Ruled out: transferring the lead's ownership when scheduling. It would have needed `canReassign` widened
to TELECALLER and would have moved the lead out of the telecaller's queue and their re-engagement lane,
contradicting "NO_SHOW reverts to telecaller". Not built.

Breaks 1-3 above are the entire job.

## Constraints

- Modify the dashboard route, plus the shared-component corrections named below. No other route changes;
  every other page keeps working.
- No charts. Explicit owner instruction.
- **Two backend changes are in scope**, both disclosed in Decision 1: the SALES_EXEC handoff edge
  (Break 2) and the exec's visit visibility (Break 3), plus the adjacent `projectId` filter bug. There is
  no longer a "no backend changes" claim to defend; the R1 version of this document asserted one and it
  was wrong.
- Role lanes and RLS are authoritative; the UI must never offer an action the API rejects with 403 or 400.
- The app ships a theme toggle — any colour needs a `dark:` variant.
- Prefer `@paalstack/react-ui` built-ins over hand-rolled UI; real backend data, never client-side-only
  filtering; friendly enum labels, never raw enum strings.
- Owner workflow: make changes, do not commit or push, show the diff first.
- **Assignment is to the VISIT; lead ownership does not transfer at scheduling** (owner ruling). The
  telecaller keeps the lead in their queue and keeps the NO_SHOW re-engagement lane. `canReassign` is
  unchanged (ADMIN/MANAGER only). Do not implement ownership transfer.
- **No chat pane on the dashboard.** Inbound customer WhatsApp messages reach the CRM
  (`webhooks.controller.ts:316`) while replies composed in the CRM never leave it (`crm.ts:504`).
  Rendering that pairing on the surface we ask staff to live in converts a dormant bug into active
  distrust. The row expansion links to the lead's own page instead.
- **Visit outcome roles (owner ruling, 2026-09-16).** Only SALES_EXEC (and MANAGER/ADMIN) mark a visit
  COMPLETED, and that is the sole path to VISITED (`visits.service.ts:477-480` drives `toState:
  'VISITED'` only on `outcome === 'COMPLETED'`). TELECALLER records NO_SHOW only. Enforce per-button
  with `canLogVisitOutcome(role, outcome)` (`lib/session.ts:145-154`). No row may offer a route to
  VISITED.
- **The generic transition endpoint still allows TELECALLER VISIT_SCHEDULED → VISITED**
  (`leads.state-machine.ts:94-105`). Tightening it is a backend change, out of scope, and a recorded
  follow-up. Stated so the gap is known, not assumed closed.

## Decision 2 (REQUIRED) — the no-403 rule against the components we reuse

Two reuse targets violate the constraint "the UI must never offer an action the API rejects". Both must
be corrected, because the dashboard's expansion renders them:

- **`LeadVisitPanel`.** `LeadVisitPanel.tsx:75` gates outcome buttons on `canScheduleVisits(role)`,
  which includes TELECALLER (`session.ts:138-142`), so it renders "Mark completed". The backend refuses
  it: `visits.state-machine.ts:55` gives TELECALLER only CANCELLED / NO_SHOW / RESCHEDULED from
  SCHEDULED, mapped to `ForbiddenException` (`visits.service.ts:441-448`). The correct helper,
  `canLogVisitOutcome`, exists and is imported nowhere in the web app — dead code. **Fix: gate each
  outcome button on `canLogVisitOutcome(role, outcome)`.**
- **`LeadActionPanel`.** Its own header documents that it exposes *all* outgoing edges and lets the
  server reject with 403 (`LeadActionPanel.tsx:12-15`), rendering the raw `TRANSITIONS` mirror
  (`:75-88`) unfiltered (`:346-362`). **Fix: the dashboard renders a role-filtered transition set.**
  If that means an optional `allowedStates` prop on `LeadActionPanel`, it must default to current
  behaviour so the lead detail page is unchanged. If the filter proves invasive, the fallback is for the
  dashboard to render its own transition buttons and leave the panel untouched.

## Decision 3 (REQUIRED) — the queue sort cannot be what R1 claimed

R1 specified four buckets: overdue NEW → NEW → VISIT_REQUESTED → VISIT_SCHEDULED today. **Buckets 3 and
4 are not implementable as stated.**

- The server sort is fixed (`leads.service.ts:286-302`): overdue NEW (0) / NEW (1) / everything else (2),
  tie-broken `updatedAt DESC, createdAt DESC`. The client may only override with `sortBy` from the
  whitelist `['updatedAt','createdAt','name']` (`crm.ts:71`, `api-types/src/leads.ts:148`). No parameter
  yields VISIT_REQUESTED before VISIT_SCHEDULED.
- "VISIT_SCHEDULED today" needs visit data the lead row does not carry at all — `LeadRow`
  (`leads.service.ts:68-81`) has no visit fields.

**Resolution, stated honestly in the build:** the queue uses the **existing server order** (overdue NEW,
then NEW, then the rest), followed by a **documented client-side refinement limited to the loaded page**
— the page size must be named in the code, and the refinement must say so in a comment rather than
pretending to be server-side. Bucket 4 is dropped from the queue; a lead with a visit today appears in
the **visits panel**, where that data actually lives. This keeps "real backend data, never
client-side-only filtering" honest by naming the limit instead of hiding it.

## Decision 4 (REQUIRED) — the age staircase is NEW-only, and R1 overclaimed

R1 said age renders on every collapsed row via `leadAgeTier` / `LEAD_AGE_TIER_CLASS` and that "the SLA
clock is never hidden". **That is false for most rows.** `leadAgeTier` returns `null` for any non-NEW
state (`lib/leads.ts:85-93`) and `isOverdue` returns false for non-NEW (`:50-63`), both pinned by tests
(`lib/leads.test.ts:135-141`). The tier also measures age since **creation**, not time-to-breach.

- NEW rows: the existing staircase, unchanged (yellow 10 min / amber 20 min / red 30 min).
- CONTACTED / VISIT_REQUESTED / VISIT_SCHEDULED / RESCHEDULED / NO_SHOW rows: **no age at all** under
  the existing helper. The build must add a second, honest staleness measure — hours since `updatedAt`,
  or a scheduled-visit time from the visits join — labelled as staleness. It must not reuse the
  creation-age staircase, which would be a fabricated deadline.
- The SLA claim narrows to what is true: the 30-minute clock is visible on the rows it governs (NEW);
  every other row shows staleness, named as staleness.

## Premises

All accepted by the owner (2026-09-16). Premise 4 was restated and premise 8 added by the owner's ruling;
premise 5 was corrected twice (first by the R2 review, then re-confirmed by the owner's visit-scheduling
ruling); premise 6 was narrowed after review; premise 10 records the ruling that closed the assignment fork.

1. The dashboard is a **work queue**, not a metrics page. Its competitor is Excel plus WhatsApp.
2. The **30-minute first-touch SLA is the organizing principle** of the NEW rows. Most urgent first *is*
   the interface — which keeps the page calm **without hiding anything a user needs on a given pass**.
   Rare, negative actions are deliberately deferred to the expansion.
3. **Ordering does the work** the original "reveal segment by segment" proposal was reaching for —
   subject to Decision 3's limits on what the server can actually sort.
4. **All three work roles are served on this page** — telecaller, sales exec, manager — differing in the
   hero panel and the permitted action on each row, not in the route. Owner-stated 2026-09-16.
5. **A telecaller can complete their entire lane in-page** — schedule a visit and assign it to an exec
   from their own team or team manager — and the exec then conducts it and moves the lead to VISITED.
   **CONFIRMED TRUE by the owner's 2026-09-16 ruling**, with three caveats found by tracing it: the exec
   picker must be fed from `/api/users/team` (frontend only), and two backend changes are required for the
   exec half (Decision 1, Breaks 2 and 3). The no-backend-change claim was wrong; the workflow is correct.
6. **Reveal happens in place** by expanding a row — never by navigating away. *Caveat accepted from the
   review: a modal is still the correct host for a multi-field form that genuinely needs one
   (scheduling), because an inline date-time plus assignee form inside a queue row is worse for a novice
   than a dialog. The rule governs reads and one-tap actions.*
7. Scope is confined to the **dashboard route plus the named shared-component corrections and the two
   disclosed backend changes**; all other pages untouched.
8. **Only a sales exec (or manager/admin) marks a visit COMPLETED, and that is the sole path to
   VISITED.** TELECALLER records NO_SHOW only. Owner ruling, 2026-09-16; matches `canLogVisitOutcome` and
   the service's `COMPLETED → VISITED` drive. **No core lifecycle design was changed by this ruling.**
9. **The chat-send and reminders defects are known, documented, and deliberately left unfixed** in this
   scope. Recorded as upgrade triggers, not silently accepted.
10. **Assignment is to the VISIT, not the lead.** The telecaller assigns the sales exec who will conduct
    the visit; lead ownership does not transfer at scheduling time, so the telecaller keeps the lead and
    Model C's NO_SHOW re-engagement lane, and `canReassign` is unchanged. Owner ruling, 2026-09-16.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Ordered queue, visible next action, expand in place (SELECTED)

Queue in server order; each row carries its primary action as a labelled button; clicking the row expands
the rest of that lead's workspace inline. Role-adapted for all three work roles. Reuses
`ScheduleVisitDialog`, `BookingApprovalDialog`, `KpiStrip` (with the additive `onClick`), and
role-filtered transitions; reuses `LeadVisitPanel` only with the Decision 2 gate fix. Effort S–M plus
Decision 1, risk low, phone-safe.

### Approach B: One action at a time (single-card stack)

One lead per screen as a large card with a single obvious button; work top to bottom. Effort M–L, risk
medium. Rejected: bossy when a telecaller needs to work an older lead out of order, and roughly twice the
cost of A.

### Approach C: Two-pane workbench (queue left, full workspace right)

Effort M, risk medium. Rejected: densest of the three, most likely to fail on a phone — and these staff
already work from a phone. Also duplicates the lead detail page's composition.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach A**, with the interaction spec and the four decisions above. Rationale: the smallest diff that
satisfies every owner constraint, survives a phone, serves all three roles from one route, and applies
the research correctly — keep the COMMON action visible, hide only the RARE one.

### Interaction spec

Route: `apps/web/src/app/[orgSlug]/projects/[projectSlug]/dashboard/page.tsx`. Keep the existing
`mounted` / `sessionPending` guard and the role branch. Replace the chart body.

**All three roles share this one page.** The route and layout are identical; role decides the queue
contents, the counts, and which action is permitted on each row.

**Telecaller view, top to bottom:**

1. **Count strip — three real numbers, not four.** *Needs a call now* (overdue count), *New today*,
   *Visits today*. Each is a **filter control**. Two corrections from review:
   - `KpiStrip` cannot do this as written: `Kpi` is `{label, value, sub}` with no handler and the markup
     is a plain paragraph grid (`dashboard-shared.tsx:20-24, 35-61`). **Fix: add an optional `onClick` to
     `Kpi`**, defaulting to current behaviour — `dashboard-shared.tsx` is also imported by the admin
     overview page (`admin/overview/page.tsx:40`), so the change must stay additive.
   - A fourth count, "Visits to confirm", is dropped: see Open Question 2. There is no data model for a
     confirmation, and a count with an undecided definition violates the honest-numbers rule.
   - Mobile: `KpiStrip` renders `grid-cols-2` with `text-3xl` values (`:37, :46`) — two tall rows before
     the queue starts. **Fix: collapse to a single scrollable chip row under `sm`**, to protect the
     "urgent lead on screen without scrolling" criterion.
3. **"Call these now"** — the queue, in **server order** plus the documented page-limited refinement
   (Decision 3). It contains NEW and CONTACTED leads, plus VISIT_REQUESTED and the re-engagement states,
   so a row the telecaller just acted on does not vanish mid-task.
3. **Row anatomy** (identifying column first, per standing preference): lead name + phone → status badge
   (friendly label) → age or staleness (Decision 4) → **primary action button**, visible without a click.
   **Name truncates** with `line-clamp-1` and a `title` attribute.
4. **Primary action by state**, telecaller lane:

   | State | Primary (on the row) | Secondary |
   |---|---|---|
   | NEW | Called (→ CONTACTED) | Wants a visit (→ VISIT_REQUESTED) |
   | CONTACTED | Wants a visit (→ VISIT_REQUESTED) | — |
   | VISIT_REQUESTED | Schedule visit (opens `ScheduleVisitDialog`, **exec required**) | — |
   | VISIT_SCHEDULED | No show | Reschedule |
   | RESCHEDULED | Schedule visit | — |
   | NO_SHOW | Schedule visit | — |

   **Scheduling must require the exec.** `ScheduleVisitDialog` is fed from `/api/users/team` filtered to
   `SALES_EXEC | MANAGER` (Decision 1, Break 1); the field is mandatory, not optional, because an absent
   `salesExecId` falls back to the telecaller and the create fails with a 400. Label the field for a
   non-technical user ("Who will show the villa?"), not "Sales exec".

   LOST and COLD are absent deliberately: rare, negative, reason-required, therefore expansion-only.
   **No row offers a route to VISITED.** Two mechanisms must be named in the code, because "No show" and
   "Reschedule" each exist twice with different effects:
   - **No show** on a row: the *lead* transition (allowed for telecallers,
     `leads.state-machine.ts:53, 103-104`). It writes `Lead` plus an audit row only, so the `SiteVisit`
     row stays SCHEDULED. **Say so in a comment**; do not imply the visit row changed.
   - **Reschedule** on a row: **must route through `POST /visits/:id/reschedule`**
     (`visits.service.ts:520-613`), not the lead transition. `DESIGN.md:98` defines RESCHEDULED as "old
     `SiteVisit` marked RESCHEDULED, new one created with `rescheduledFromId`", and the lead transition
     alone does not do that. A RESCHEDULED lead with a live SCHEDULED visit can otherwise be scheduled
     again (`create()` accepts RESCHEDULED at `visits.service.ts:217`, the dialog permits it at
     `ScheduleVisitDialog.tsx:33`, and auto-advance only fires for VISIT_REQUESTED), producing two open
     visits.
5. **Expansion, in place.** Clicking the row expands it, revealing tap-to-call phone, status, owner,
   **role-filtered transitions** (Decision 2), `LeadVisitPanel` (with the Decision 2 gate fix), and a link
   to the lead's own page. One row expanded at a time.
   - **The expanded row must survive a refetch.** Every mutation invalidates `['leads']`
     (`crm.ts:238-241`, `:268-271`, `:409-416`), so the queue re-sorts while the row is open and, with an
     urgency sort, the row moves mid-interaction. **Fix: pin the expanded row** until the user collapses
     it, and state what happens when the acted-on lead leaves the queue — keep it pinned with a "no
     longer in this queue" note rather than yanking it.
   - **Known limitations to state in code, not hide:**
     - `LeadVisitPanel` resolves its open visit via `useVisits({})` (`:65`), which takes the default

1. **RESOLVED (owner ruling 2026-09-16).** Assignment is to the VISIT, not the lead: the exec conducts

   changed.**
3. **"Confirm a visit" has no data representation.** Visit status is
   SCHEDULED / RESCHEDULED / COMPLETED / NO_SHOW / CANCELLED. The telecaller's 24h and 2h confirmation
   calls — a named part of their job — leave no trace. **Until decided, the fourth count does not ship**;
   the strip carries three numbers whose definitions are settled.
4. **Primary device unverified.** The spec assumes phone-first. `WIREFRAMES.md` #2 defines a mobile
   manager dashboard and #4 defines the telecaller's home as the inbox, so mobile is expected, but nobody
   has watched it. The observation settles this.
5. **Chat-send ceiling (accepted debt).** Scope A fixes page-hopping and leaves the duplicate typing.
   Marked in code at the point of the cut as `gstack-shortcut(dec-office-hours-D4A): scope A, upgrade when
   a telecaller reports retyping the same message twice`.
6. **Lead identifier for the queue.** The standing preference is unit/villa number first, name second —
   that is a booking's identifier. A lead at NEW has no unit yet, so the queue uses name + phone. Confirm
   that matches how telecallers look a lead up.
7. **Progressive reveal itself is unvalidated.** This design bets that expanding a row is how a novice
   works a queue. With instrumentation struck from scope, the observation must be done on paper from day
   one: count expands per lead worked, by hand. If expands are near zero, the pattern is wrong and rows
   should show all actions collapsed by default.
8. **Density is unproven.** If a telecaller's queue routinely exceeds ~30 rows, the queue needs a filter
   default (overdue-only) rather than a longer scroll. Decide from the observation.
9. **Staleness measure for non-NEW rows (Decision 4) is a product choice.** Hours since `updatedAt` is the
